Active & Passive Voice

Master the art of voice transformation with comprehensive examples and practice

📚 Introduction

What is Active Voice?

In Active Voice, the subject performs the action. The sentence follows the pattern: Subject + Verb + Object

Active voice mein subject kaam karta hai. Subject verb se pehle aata hai.
Example: Ram eats an apple. (Ram is doing the action of eating)

What is Passive Voice?

In Passive Voice, the subject receives the action. The object becomes the subject, and we use a form of "be" + past participle.

Passive voice mein subject kaam receive karta hai. Object subject ban jata hai.
Example: An apple is eaten by Ram. (Apple is receiving the action)

🔍 Key Differences

Aspect Active Voice Passive Voice
Subject Position At the beginning At the end (with 'by') or omitted
Object Position At the end At the beginning
Verb Form Simple verb form Be + Past Participle
Focus On the doer On the action/recipient
Structure Subject + Verb + Object Object + Be + PP + (by + Subject)

📏 Rules & Formulas for All Tenses

🟢 Present Tenses

Simple Present

Active: Subject + V1/V5 + Object
Passive: Object + is/am/are + V3 + (by + Subject)
Active: She writes a letter.
Passive: A letter is written by her.

Present Continuous

Active: Subject + is/am/are + V1+ing + Object
Passive: Object + is/am/are + being + V3 + (by + Subject)
Active: They are playing cricket.
Passive: Cricket is being played by them.

Present Perfect

Active: Subject + has/have + V3 + Object
Passive: Object + has/have + been + V3 + (by + Subject)
Active: I have completed the work.
Passive: The work has been completed by me.

Present Perfect Continuous

⚠️ No Passive Form: This tense cannot be converted to passive voice because it emphasizes duration of ongoing action.

🔵 Past Tenses

Simple Past

Active: Subject + V2 + Object
Passive: Object + was/were + V3 + (by + Subject)
Active: He cooked dinner.
Passive: Dinner was cooked by him.

Past Continuous

Active: Subject + was/were + V1+ing + Object
Passive: Object + was/were + being + V3 + (by + Subject)
Active: She was reading a book.
Passive: A book was being read by her.

Past Perfect

Active: Subject + had + V3 + Object
Passive: Object + had + been + V3 + (by + Subject)
Active: They had finished the project.
Passive: The project had been finished by them.

Past Perfect Continuous

⚠️ No Passive Form: This tense emphasizes duration and cannot be passivized.

🟡 Future Tenses

Simple Future

Active: Subject + will/shall + V1 + Object
Passive: Object + will/shall + be + V3 + (by + Subject)
Active: I will send the email.
Passive: The email will be sent by me.

Future Continuous

⚠️ No Passive Form: Future continuous is rarely used in passive voice.

Future Perfect

Active: Subject + will/shall + have + V3 + Object
Passive: Object + will/shall + have + been + V3 + (by + Subject)
Active: She will have completed the task.
Passive: The task will have been completed by her.

Future Perfect Continuous

⚠️ No Passive Form: This tense is not used in passive voice.

📝 Step-by-Step Transformation

How to Convert Active to Passive:

  1. Identify the object in the active sentence
  2. Make the object the new subject of the passive sentence
  3. Change the verb to appropriate tense of "be" + past participle
  4. Add "by" + original subject at the end (if necessary)
  5. Remove "by whom" when the doer is unknown or unimportant
Example Transformation:
Active: The teacher explains the lesson.
Step 1: Object = "the lesson"
Step 2: New subject = "The lesson"
Step 3: Verb = "is explained" (Simple Present)
Step 4: Add "by the teacher"
Passive: The lesson is explained by the teacher.

❌ Common Mistakes

1. Wrong Verb Form

Wrong: The letter is wrote by her.

Correct: The letter is written by her.

Why: Use past participle (V3) form, not base form.

2. Forgetting "by"

Wrong: The cake was baked Mary.

Correct: The cake was baked by Mary.

Why: "By" is necessary to show the doer of the action.

3. Wrong Tense Conversion

Wrong: The work is completed by him. (for past action)

Correct: The work was completed by him.

Why: Match the tense with the original active sentence.

4. Trying to Passive Non-passive Tenses

Wrong: The food was being cooked by her for two hours.

Correct: She had been cooking food for two hours. (Keep active)

Why: Perfect continuous tenses don't have passive forms.
