Determiners

Grammar Learning Hub

Introduction

Determiners are words placed in front of a noun to specify quantity (e.g., "one dog," "many dogs") or to clarify what the noun refers to (e.g., "my dog," "that dog," "the dog").

📌 Hinglish Explanation:
Determiners wo shabd hote hain jo noun se pehle aakar ye tay karte hain ki hum kis cheez ki baat kar rahe hain ya wo kitni matra mein hai.

Determiners vs. Adjectives

Adjectives describe the quality (e.g., big house), while Determiners introduce the noun (e.g., this house).

Position Rule:
Determiner + (Adjective) + Noun
Example: The (Det) black (Adj) cat (Noun).

Types of Determiners

1. Articles (a, an, the)

▼

Articles define whether a noun is specific or unspecific.

  • Indefinite (a, an): Used for general, singular, countable nouns.
  • Definite (the): Used for specific nouns (singular/plural/uncountable).
👉 Hinglish:
'A/An' tab use karein jab baat general ho (ek ladka). 'The' tab use karein jab baat specific ho (wahi ladka).

Common Mistakes

❌ He is an honest.
Adjective alone cannot take an article.
✔ He is an honest man.
❌ I saw a elephant.
✔ I saw an elephant. (Vowel sound)

2. Demonstratives (this, that, these, those)

▼

These point to specific things based on distance and number.

WordDistanceNumber
ThisNearSingular
ThatFarSingular
TheseNearPlural
ThoseFarPlural
👉 Hinglish:
Paas ke liye 'This/These' aur door ke liye 'That/Those'.

3. Possessives (my, your, his, her, etc.)

▼

These show ownership or relationship. They always come before a noun.

List: My, Your, His, Her, Its, Our, Their.

❌ The book is my.
'My' is a determiner, it needs a noun.
✔ This is my book. / The book is mine.

4. Quantifiers (some, any, much, many...)

▼

Quantifiers express the amount or quantity of a noun.

Countable vs Uncountable Rule:
• Countable: Many, Few, A few, Several
• Uncountable: Much, Little, A little, A bit of
• Both: Some, Any, All, A lot of
👉 Some vs Any:
'Some' positive sentences me use hota hai. 'Any' negative ya question me use hota hai.

5. Numbers (Cardinal & Ordinal)

▼
  • Cardinal (How many): One, two, ten, fifty.
  • Ordinal (Order/Rank): First, second, last, next.

Order Rule: Ordinals come before Cardinals.
Example: The first two chapters (Not 'The two first').

6. Distributives (each, every, either...)

▼

Used to refer to members of a group individually.

  • Each: Individual focus (Two or more).
  • Every: Group focus (Three or more).
  • Either: One or the other (of two).
  • Neither: None of the two.
❌ Every students passed.
✔ Every student passed. (Always singular noun)

7. Interrogative Determiners

▼

Used to ask questions. Must be followed by a noun.

Words: Which, What, Whose.

Example: Which color do you like?
