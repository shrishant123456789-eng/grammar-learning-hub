Pronouns

Grammar Learning Hub

Introduction

A Pronoun is a word used in place of a Noun. It helps us avoid repeating the same noun over and over again, making sentences sound natural.

📌 Hinglish Explanation:
Pronoun (Sarvnaam) wo shabd hai jo Noun (Sangya) ki jagah par use hota hai. Taaki humein ek hi naam baar-baar na bolna pade.

Examples

  • Rahul is absent because he is ill. (He = Rahul)
  • The books are where you left them. (Them = The books)

Types of Pronouns

1. Personal Pronouns

▼

These represent specific people or things. They have two main forms: Subject (doer of action) and Object (receiver of action).

PersonSubject (Doer)Object (Receiver)
1st (Speaker)I, WeMe, Us
2nd (Listener)YouYou
3rd (Other)He, She, It, TheyHim, Her, It, Them
👉 Rule: Agar Pronoun sentence shuru kare toh 'Subject Form' use karein. Agar verb ke baad aaye toh 'Object Form' use karein.
❌ Him and me went to the market.
✔ He and I went to the market.
(Use Subject form)

2. Possessive Pronouns

▼

These show ownership. They stand alone and do not need a noun after them.

List: Mine, Ours, Yours, His, Hers, Theirs.

Difference:
This is my book. (Adjective - needs noun)
This book is mine. (Pronoun - stands alone)
❌ That car is hers car.
✔ That car is hers.

3. Reflexive Pronouns

▼

Used when the Subject and Object are the same person. They end in -self or -selves.

List: Myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves.

👉 Hinglish: Jab koi insaan koi kaam "khud par" ya "apne aap" karta hai.
❌ Myself Shrishant Yadav. (Intro)
✔ I am Shrishant Yadav.
(Reflexive pronoun cannot be a Subject)

4. Demonstrative Pronouns

▼

Used to point at specific things.

  • This / That: Singular
  • These / Those: Plural

Note: If a noun follows immediately (e.g., "This car"), it is an adjective. If it stands alone (e.g., "This is a car"), it is a pronoun.

5. Relative Pronouns

▼

Used to join clauses or relate to a noun mentioned earlier.

WordUse
Who/WhomFor People
WhichFor Things/Animals
ThatFor People or Things
WhosePossession
❌ The dog who bit me.
✔ The dog which/that bit me.

6. Interrogative Pronouns

▼

Used to ask questions.

List: Who, Whom, Whose, Which, What.

👉 Example:
Who is there? (Subject)
Whom did you meet? (Object)

7. Indefinite Pronouns

▼

Refer to people or things in a general way, not specific.

Singular: Everyone, someone, nobody, anything, something.
Plural: Many, few, several, both.

❌ Everyone are present.
✔ Everyone is present.
('Everyone' is grammatically singular)

8. Distributive Pronouns

▼

Refer to persons or things one at a time. They are always Singular.

List: Each, Either, Neither.

👉 Use:
Either: Do mein se ek.
Neither: Do mein se koi nahi.
❌ Each of the boys have a pen.
✔ Each of the boys has a pen.

9. Reciprocal Pronouns

▼

Used when two or more people do the same thing to each other.

  • Each other: For two people.
  • One another: For more than two.
