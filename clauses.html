Clauses

Master Sentence Building Blocks: Noun, Adjective & Adverb Clauses

1. Introduction to Clauses

What is a Clause?
A clause is a group of words that contains a Subject and a Verb. It is the fundamental building block of a sentence.
Clause words ka wo group hota hai jisme apna Subject aur Verb hota hai. Ye Phrase se alag hota hai kyunki Phrase mein subject+verb combination nahi hota.

Clause vs Phrase

Phrase (No Subject-Verb) Clause (Has Subject-Verb)
In the morning When the sun rises
A beautiful girl Who is beautiful

Simple Examples:

  • I eat. (1 Clause: Subject 'I', Verb 'eat')
  • She wept because she was sad. (2 Clauses)
2️⃣ Independent (Main) Clause

Also called the Principal Clause. It expresses a complete thought and can stand alone as a sentence.

Ye sentence ka wo hissa hai jo apne aap mein pura sentence ban sakta hai. Isse kisi aur sahare ki zarurat nahi hoti.

Examples:

  • The sun rises in the east.
  • I like coffee, but my brother likes tea.
  • She is a doctor.
3️⃣ Dependent (Subordinate) Clause

It has a subject and a verb but does not express a complete thought. It depends on the Main Clause.

Ye akela nahi aa sakta. Iska meaning tabhi clear hoga jab ye Main Clause ke saath jude. Usually ye words se shuru hota hai jaise: because, if, when, that, which.

Examples:

  • ... because she was late. (Incomplete)
  • ... if it rains today. (Incomplete)
  • ... who stole my watch. (Incomplete)
4️⃣ Noun Clause (The "What" Clause)
Definition: A dependent clause that acts as a NOUN. It can be a Subject, Object, or Complement.
Identify karne ka trick: Verb se poocho "WHAT?" (Kya?). Agar jawab pura sentence ho, to wo Noun Clause hai.
Example: "I know that he is honest."
I know WHAT? -> That he is honest.

Functions & Examples:

Function Example
Subject What he said was true.
Object I know where he lives.
Object of Preposition Listen to what I say.
Complement The problem is that we have no money.

Common Errors:

❌ Tell me where is the station.

✔ Tell me where the station is.

👉 Why? Noun clauses use statement order (Subject + Verb), not question order.

5️⃣ Adjective (Relative) Clause
Definition: A clause that describes or modifies a Noun or Pronoun. It starts with a Relative Pronoun (who, which, that, whose).
Jaise Adjective noun ki visheshta batata hai, waise hi ye pura clause noun ke baare mein extra info deta hai.
Ex: The boy who is playing is my brother. (Kaunsa ladka? Jo khel raha hai.)

Relative Pronouns:

  • Who / Whom: For people.
  • Which: For things/animals.
  • That: For people or things (Defining only).
  • Whose: Possession.

Defining vs Non-Defining:

  • Defining (No Commas): Essential info.
    The car which I bought is red.
  • Non-Defining (Commas): Extra info.
    My father, who is 60, likes cricket.

Common Mistakes:

❌ The man which called me.

✔ The man who called me.

👉 'Man' is a person, so use 'who', not 'which'.

6️⃣ Adverb Clause
Definition: Acts as an Adverb. It modifies a Verb, Adjective, or another Adverb. It answers: When? Where? Why? How? Under what condition?

Types & Examples:

TypeConjunctionsExample
Timewhen, while, beforeI will call when I arrive.
Placewhere, whereverGo where you like.
Reasonbecause, since, asI slept because I was tired.
Conditionif, unlessIf it rains, we will cancel.
Concessionalthough, thoughAlthough he is poor, he is happy.
7️⃣ Reduced Clauses (Advanced)

We can shorten Adjective and Adverb clauses to make sentences concise.

Adjective Clause Reduction:

Remove the relative pronoun and 'be' verb.

  • Full: The man who is standing there is my uncle.
  • Reduced: The man standing there is my uncle.
  • Full: The letter which was written by him.
  • Reduced: The letter written by him.

8. Clause Identification Tricks ⚡

1. The "What" Test (Noun Clause): Replace the clause with "SOMETHING". If it makes sense, it's a Noun Clause.

  • I know what you did. -> I know SOMETHING. (Makes sense = Noun Clause)

2. The "Which One" Test (Adjective Clause): Does it tell you WHICH noun we are talking about?

  • The pen that I lost. (Which pen? The lost one = Adjective Clause)

3. The "Mover" Test (Adverb Clause): Adverb clauses can usually move to the start of the sentence without changing meaning.

  • I will eat when I get home. = When I get home, I will eat. (Adverb Clause)
