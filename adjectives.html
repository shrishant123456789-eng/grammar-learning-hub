Adjectives

Grammar Learning Hub

What is an Adjective?

An adjective is a word that describes or modifies a noun or a pronoun. It adds more information about the quality, quantity, number, or state of the noun.

Hinglish Explanation:
Adjective wo shabd hota hai jo kisi Noun (Sangya) ya Pronoun (Sarvnaam) ki visheshta batata hai. Jaise: Accha, Bura, Lamba, Kala, etc.

Why are they important?

Without adjectives, our language would be boring and vague. They help paint a picture in the listener's mind.

Examples:
  • She is a smart girl. (Describes 'girl')
  • I have three pens. (Describes number of 'pens')
  • The tea is hot. (Describes state of 'tea')

Types of Adjectives

1. Adjectives of Quality

▼

Definition: Describes the kind, quality, or state of a person or thing.

Ye batata hai ki koi cheez ya insaan kaisa hai (Rang, roop, gun).
Question to ask: "Of what kind?" (Kaisa?)
CategoryExamples
SizeBig, small, tall, short
GeneralGood, bad, beautiful, honest
ColorRed, blue, black, golden

Structure & Mistakes

❌ She is a beautiful.
Error: Missing noun/article structure.
✔ She is beautiful.
OR
✔ She is a beautiful girl.

2. Adjectives of Quantity

▼

Definition: Indicates the approximate amount of something. Used mostly with Uncountable Nouns.

Ye batata hai ki koi cheez kitni hai (Maatra). Iska use un cheezo ke liye hota hai jinhe gina nahi ja sakta (Uncountable).
Question to ask: "How much?" (Kitna?)

Common Words: Some, any, little, much, enough, whole, all.

❌ I have many rice.
Rice is uncountable.
✔ I have much rice.

3. Adjectives of Number

▼

Definition: Shows how many persons or things are meant, or in what order.

Ye batata hai cheezein kitni hain (Sankhya). Use with Countable Nouns.
Question to ask: "How many?" (Kitne?)
  • Definite: One, two, first, second.
  • Indefinite: Many, few, some, several.
❌ I have much friends.
Friends can be counted.
✔ I have many friends.

4. Demonstrative Adjectives

▼

Definition: Points out which person or thing is meant.

Ye ishara karne ke liye use hota hai. (Yeh, Woh). Note: Iske turant baad Noun aana zaroori hai.
Example: This car is mine.
SingularPlural
This (Near)These (Near)
That (Far)Those (Far)
❌ This are my books.
'This' is singular, 'books' is plural.
✔ These are my books.

5. Possessive Adjectives

▼

Definition: Shows ownership or possession.

Ye batata hai ki cheez kiski hai. (Mera, Tera, Uska).
Words: My, your, his, her, its, our, their.

Structure: Possessive Adjective + Noun

✔ This is my house.

❌ The book is my.
'My' needs a noun after it. Use 'mine' (pronoun) for this structure.
✔ This is my book. / The book is mine.

6. Interrogative Adjectives

▼

Definition: Used with nouns to ask questions.

Sawal poochne ke liye use hota hai, aur iske turant baad Noun aata hai.
Words: Which, What, Whose.
  • Which way shall we go?
  • Whose pen is this?

7. Distributive Adjectives

▼

Definition: Refers to each one of a number.

Ye group ke har ek member ko alag-alag darshata hai. Ye hamesha Singular treat hote hain.
Words: Each, Every, Either, Neither.
❌ Every students passed.
'Every' takes a singular noun.
✔ Every student passed.

8. Proper Adjectives

▼

Definition: Formed from Proper Nouns (names of specific places or people).

Kisi jagah ya naam se bana hua adjective. Hamesha Capital letter se shuru hota hai.
Example: India -> Indian, Shakespeare -> Shakespearean.

I love Indian food.

She likes Chinese culture.

9. Compound Adjectives

▼

Definition: Formed by combining two or more words, usually connected by a hyphen.

Jab do shabd milkar ek adjective ka kaam karein.
Examples: Good-looking, Part-time, Five-year-old.
❌ I saw a five-years-old boy.
Adjectives generally do not take plural 's'.
✔ I saw a five-year-old boy.
