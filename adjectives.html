① WHAT IS AN ADJECTIVE?

📚 Definition

An adjective is a word that describes or modifies a noun or pronoun. It tells us more about a person, place, thing, or idea.

🇮🇳 Hinglish Explanation

Adjective wo shabd hota hai jo noun ya pronoun ke baare mein adhik jaankari deta hai. Ye batata hai ki koi cheez kaisi hai, kitni hai, ya kaunsi hai.

🧮 Formula

Adjective → Describes Noun / Pronoun

✅ Correct Examples

Beautiful girl (Kaisi ladki?)

Five books (Kitni books?)

This pen (Kaunsi pen?)

Tall building (Kaisa building?)

Indian food (Kaisa food?)

❌ Wrong Examples

✗ Beautiful quickly ran. (Adjective verb ko modify kar raha - wrong!)

✗ She is beautifully. (Adjective adverb ki jagah - wrong!)

✗ Very beautiful girl. (Very adverb hai, adjective nahin!)

② IDENTIFICATION TRICK (पहचान)

🔍 Test Questions

Adjective pahchanne ke liye ye sawal pucho:

  • कैसा? (Kaisa?) - Quality
  • कितना? (Kitna?) - Quantity
  • कौनसा? (Kaunsa?) - Demonstrative
  • कितने? (Kitne?) - Number
Sentence Test Question Adjective Type

Five books कितनी किताबें? Five Number

SSC TIP: Agar koi word inme se koi bhi sawal ka jawab de, to wo adjective hai!

④ ADJECTIVE OF QUALITY

📖 Definition

Adjectives of quality describe the nature, kind, or characteristic of a noun or pronoun. They tell us what kind of person or thing it is.

🇮🇳 Hinglish Explanation

Quality ke adjectives noun ya pronoun ki quality, nature ya characteristic batate hain. Ye dikhate hain ki wo kaisa hai.

✅ Key Rules

  • Answer the question "कैसा?" (Kaisa?)
  • Can be opinion (beautiful, ugly) or fact (round, square)
  • Can have degrees (big, bigger, biggest)
  • Most common type of adjective

✅ Examples

Beautiful girl

Tall building

Honest man

Red car

Indian food

Round table

❌ Common Mistakes

Wrong: She is beautiful girl. (Article missing)

Right: She is a beautiful girl.

Why wrong: Singular countable noun ke sahe hamesha article lagta hai!

Wrong: He is very honest man. (Article missing)

Right: He is a very honest man.

Why wrong: Adjective ke baad bhi noun ke rules follow karne hote hain!

SSC TIP: Quality adjectives hamesha "कैसा?" ka jawab dete hain. Ye sabse common hote hain!

⑤ ADJECTIVE OF QUANTITY

📖 Definition

Adjectives of quantity show how much or how many of something there is. They are used with uncountable nouns.

🇮🇳 Hinglish Explanation

Quantity ke adjectives batate hain ki kisi cheez ki kitni matra hai. Ye uncountable nouns ke saath use hote hain.

✅ Key Rules

  • Answer the question "कितना?" (Kitna?)
  • Used with uncountable nouns (water, rice, money)
  • Much vs Many: Much = uncountable, Many = countable
  • Little/A little/The little have different meanings

✅ Much vs Many

Much water (uncountable)

Many books (countable)

Some rice (positive)

Any sugar (question/negative)

✅ Little Usage

Little water (almost none - negative)

A little water (some - positive)

The little water (jo thoda hai wo)

❌ Common Mistakes

Wrong: I have much books. (Much with countable)

Right: I have many books.

Why wrong: 'Books' countable hai - 'many' use karna chahiye!

Wrong: She has many money. (Many with uncountable)

Right: She has much money.

Why wrong: 'Money' uncountable hai - 'much' ya 'a lot of' use karein!

Wrong: I don't have some money. (Some in negative)

Right: I don't have any money.

Why: Negative sentences mein 'any' use karte hain, 'some' nahin!

SSC TIP: Much = uncountable, Many = countable. Negative mein 'any' positive mein 'some'!

⑥ ADJECTIVE OF NUMBER

📖 Definition

Adjectives of number show how many persons or things there are. They are used with countable nouns.

🇮🇳 Hinglish Explanation

Number ke adjectives batate hain ki kitne log ya cheezein hain. Ye countable nouns ke saath use hote hain.

✅ Key Rules

  • Answer the question "कितने?" (Kitne?)
  • Used with countable nouns (books, students, chairs)
  • Few/A few/The few have different meanings
  • Definite numbers: one, two, three, first, second
  • Indefinite numbers: some, many, several, all

✅ Definite Numbers

Five students

Third chapter

Both boys

All members

✅ Few Usage

Few students (almost none - negative)

A few students (some - positive)

The few students (jo kuch hain wo)

Several people (undefined number)

❌ Common Mistakes

Wrong: I have few money. (Few with uncountable)

Right: I have little money.

Why wrong: 'Money' uncountable hai - 'little' use karna chahiye!

Wrong: She has a few water. (A few with uncountable)

Right: She has a little water.

Why wrong: Uncountable nouns ke sahe 'little' family use karte hain!

Wrong: Both of them is coming. (Wrong verb)

Right: Both of them are coming.

Why: 'Both' hamesha plural concept hota hai - plural verb!

SSC TIP: Few = countable, Little = uncountable. 'Both' hamesha plural verb ke saath aata hai!

⑦ DEMONSTRATIVE ADJECTIVES

📖 Definition

Demonstrative adjectives point out which person or thing is meant. They show distance in space or time.

🇮🇳 Hinglish Explanation

Demonstrative adjectives batate hain ki kaunsi person ya thing ki baat ho rahi hai. Ye space ya time mein distance dikhate hain.

✅ Key Rules

  • This: Near, singular (ye - paas, ek)
  • That: Far, singular (wo - door, ek)
  • These: Near, plural (ye - paas, bahut)
  • Those: Far, plural (wo - door, bahut)
  • Hamesha noun ke pehle aate hain
  • Distance physical ya time dono ho sakti hai

✅ Distance Examples

This book is mine. (Paas)

That building is tall. (Door)

These shoes are new. (Paas, bahut)

Those mountains are high. (Door, bahut)

✅ Time Examples

This week is busy. (Current)

That day was special. (Past)

These days are hard. (Current days)

Those were the days! (Past days)

❌ Common Mistakes

Wrong: This books are new. (Singular adjective with plural noun)

Right: These books are new.

Why wrong: Adjective aur noun ka number match karna important hai!

Wrong: Those is my friend. (Plural adjective with singular noun)

Right: That is my friend.

Why wrong: 'Those' plural hai, 'friend' singular - mismatch!

Wrong: This are my shoes. (Wrong verb)

Right: These are my shoes.

Why: Adjective, noun, aur verb teenon ka number match hona chahiye!

SSC TIP: This/That = singular, These/Those = plural. Aur hamesha noun ke pehle aate hain!

⑧ POSSESSIVE ADJECTIVES

📖 Definition

Possessive adjectives show ownership or possession. They always come before a noun.

🇮🇳 Hinglish Explanation

Possessive adjectives ownership ya kabza dikhate hain. Ye hamesha noun ke pehle aate hain.

✅ Key Rules

  • Hamesha noun ke pehle aate hain
  • My, your, his, her, its, our, their
  • Its vs It's: "Its" = possessive, "It's" = it is
  • His - dono adjective aur pronoun ka kaam kar sakta hai
  • Her - adjective (possessive) aur pronoun (object) dono

✅ Possessive Adjectives

My book

Your pen

His car

Her dress

Its color

Their house

✅ vs Possessive Pronouns

My book (Adjective)

Mine (Pronoun)

Your pen (Adjective)

Yours (Pronoun)

His car (Both)

Hers (Pronoun)

❌ Common Mistakes

Wrong: Its a nice day. (Confusion between its and it's)

Right: It's a nice day.

Why wrong: "It's" = "It is", "Its" = possessive form!

Wrong: This is yours book. (Double possession)

Right: This is your book. OR This book is yours.

Why wrong: 'Yours' mein already possessive meaning hai!

Wrong: There book is new. (Wrong word)

Right: Their book is new.

Why: 'There' = place, 'Their' = possession!

SSC TIP: Possessive adjectives hamesha noun ke pehle aate hain. Agar noun nahin hai to possessive pronoun use karo!

⑨ DEGREES OF COMPARISON

📖 Definition

Degrees of comparison show the relative quality of things. There are three degrees: Positive, Comparative, and Superlative.

🇮🇳 Hinglish Explanation

Comparison ke degrees cheezon ki relative quality dikhate hain. Teen degrees hote hain: Positive, Comparative, aur Superlative.

📊 Degrees of Comparison

Degree Use Example Rule
Positive Simple quality Tall, Big, Good No comparison
Comparative Two things compare Taller, Bigger, Better +er or More
Superlative More than two Tallest, Biggest, Best +est or Most

✅ Formation Rules

  • Regular: Tall → Taller → Tallest
  • +er/+est: Short words (1-2 syllables)
  • More/Most: Long words (3+ syllables)
  • Irregular: Good → Better → Best
  • Double forms: Clever → Cleverer/More clever

✅ Regular Examples

Big → Bigger → Biggest

Small → Smaller → Smallest

Fast → Faster → Fastest

Large → Larger → Largest

✅ More/Most Examples

Beautiful → More beautiful → Most beautiful

Intelligent → More intelligent → Most intelligent

Expensive → More expensive → Most expensive

✅ Irregular Examples

Good → Better → Best

Bad → Worse → Worst

Little → Less → Least

Much → More → Most

❌ Common Mistakes

Wrong: He is more taller than me. (Double comparison)

Right: He is taller than me.

Why wrong: Double comparison nahin karte - ya to '-er' ya 'more'!

Wrong: This is most best option. (Double superlative)

Right: This is the best option.

Why wrong: 'Most' aur 'best' dono superlative hain - sirf ek use karo!

Wrong: She is beautifuler than her sister. (Wrong formation)

Right: She is more beautiful than her sister.

Why: 3+ syllable words mein 'more'/'most' use karte hain!

SSC TIP: Never use double comparison! Ya to -er/-est ya more/most, kabhi dono nahin!

⑩ POSITION OF ADJECTIVE (ORDER RULE)

📖 Definition

When we use multiple adjectives before a noun, they follow a specific order. This is called the OSASCOMP rule.

🇮🇳 Hinglish Explanation

Jab hum ek se zyada adjectives noun ke pehle use karte hain, to ye specific order mein aate hain. Ise OSASCOMP rule kehte hain.

🔤 OSASCOMP Rule

O - Opinion (beautiful, ugly)

S - Size (big, small)

A - Age (old, young)

S - Shape (round, square)

C - Color (red, blue)

O - Origin (Indian, American)

M - Material (wooden, metal)

P - Purpose (sleeping, running)

✅ Correct Order

A beautiful small old round red Indian wooden sleeping bed

An ugly big new square blue American metal running shoe

The lovely little ancient oval black Chinese silk wedding dress

✅ Simple Examples

Big red ball (Size + Color)

Old Indian man (Age + Origin)

Beautiful young girl (Opinion + Age)

Small round table (Size + Shape)

❌ Common Mistakes

Wrong: Red big ball (Color before size)

Right: Big red ball (Size before color)

Why wrong: Size hamesha color ke pehle aata hai!

Wrong: Indian old man (Origin before age)

Right: Old Indian man (Age before origin)

Why wrong: Age origin ke pehle aata hai!

Wrong: Wooden beautiful table (Material before opinion)

Right: Beautiful wooden table (Opinion before material)

Why: Opinion hamesha sabse pehle aata hai!

SSC TIP: Opinion sabse pehle, purpose sabse aakhir! Yaad rakho: "A beautiful big old round red Indian wooden sleeping bed"

⑪ COMMON ERRORS & EXAM TRAPS

❌ Wrong ✔ Correct Why Wrong?
He is more taller He is taller Double comparison - ya to -er ya more
This is most best This is the best Double superlative - sirf 'best' use karo
She is beautifuler She is more beautiful 3+ syllables mein 'more' use karo
I have much books I have many books Much = uncountable, Many = countable
These book is new This book is new Number mismatch - adjective aur noun match karo
Between you and I Between you and me Preposition ke baad object pronoun
Each students are Each student is Each singular hai - singular noun aur verb
Red big ball Big red ball Wrong adjective order - size before color
SSC EXAM TRAP: Much/Many confusion, double comparison, adjective order, number mismatch - ye top 4 mistakes hain!
