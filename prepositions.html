Prepositions (Relation Words)

Master the art of showing relationships between words

📚 Introduction

What are Prepositions?

A preposition is a word that shows the relationship between a noun (or pronoun) and other words in a sentence. It tells us about place, time, direction, manner, or cause.

Preposition ek aisa shabd hai jo noun/pronoun ka doosre shabdon ke saath relation batata hai. Ye batata hai ki cheez kahan hai, kab hai, kis direction mein hai, kaise hai, ya kyun hai.
Examples:
• The book is on the table. (place)
• I will meet you at 5 PM. (time)
• He walked towards the door. (direction)
• She came by car. (manner)

🔧 Types of Prepositions

A) Prepositions of Place ▼

What are Prepositions of Place?

These prepositions show where something is located or where something happens.

Ye prepositions batate hain ki koi cheez kahan hai ya kahan ho rahi hai.
in
on
at
under
over
above
below
between
among
behind
in front of
beside

Rules & Usage:

  • In: Inside something (kisi cheez ke andar)
  • On: Surface par (kisi cheez ke upar)
  • At: Exact point (kisi specific jagah par)
  • Under/Below: Niche (kisi cheez ke neeche)
  • Over/Above: Upar (kisi cheez ke upar)
  • Between: Do cheezon ke beech
  • Among: Kai cheezon ke beech

Examples:

In: The pen is in the drawer.
On: The book is on the table.
At: I am at the bus stop.
Under: The cat is under the bed.
Between: The school is between the hospital and the market.
Among: She was sitting among her friends.
Behind: The garden is behind the house.
Beside: He sat beside me in class.
B) Prepositions of Time ▼

What are Prepositions of Time?

These prepositions show when something happens or for how long.

Ye prepositions batate hain ki koi cheez kab hoti hai ya kitne samay tak hoti hai.
at
on
in
before
after
since
for
during
by
until
from...to

Rules & Usage:

  • At: Exact time (3 o'clock, noon, midnight)
  • On: Specific days/dates (Monday, 15th August)
  • In: Months, years, seasons, parts of day
  • Since: Starting point of time
  • For: Duration of time
  • During: Within a period
  • By: Not later than

Examples:

At: The meeting is at 3 PM.
On: My birthday is on Monday.
In: I was born in 1995.
Before: Come before 5 o'clock.
After: We met after the class.
Since: I have lived here since 2020.
For: I waited for two hours.
During: Don't talk during the movie.
C) Prepositions of Direction / Movement ▼

What are Prepositions of Direction?

These prepositions show movement or direction from one place to another.

Ye prepositions movement ya direction batate hain - ek jagah se doosri jagah jaana.
to
into
onto
from
towards
across
through
over
under
along

Rules & Usage:

  • To: Kisi jagah ki taraf jaana
  • Into: Andar jaana
  • Onto: Kisi surface par chadhna
  • From: Kisi jagah se aana
  • Towards: Kisi direction mein jaana
  • Across: Paar kar jaana
  • Through: Se hokar jaana

Examples:

To: I am going to the market.
Into: He jumped into the pool.
Onto: The cat climbed onto the roof.
From: She came from school.
Towards: He walked towards me.
Across: We swam across the river.
Through: The train passed through the tunnel.
Along: We walked along the beach.
D) Prepositions of Manner / Cause / Purpose ▼

What are Prepositions of Manner?

These prepositions show how something is done, why it happens, or with what it is done.

Ye prepositions batate hain ki koi cheez kaise hoti hai, kyun hoti hai, ya kiske saath hoti hai.
by
with
without
because of
due to
for
about
of

Rules & Usage:

  • By: Method or agent (kaise, kiske dwara)
  • With: Instrument or accompaniment (kiske saath)
  • Because of: Reason (kyunki)
  • Due to: Cause (ke karan)
  • For: Purpose (kiske liye)
  • About: Concerning (ke baare mein)

Examples:

By: I go to work by bus.
With: She cut the paper with scissors.
Without: He came without money.
Because of: The match was cancelled because of rain.
Due to: The delay was due to traffic.
For: This medicine is good for fever.
About: I am thinking about my future.
Of: The color of her dress is beautiful.

🤔 Most Confusing Prepositions

1. In / On / At (Place & Time)

Preposition Place Time Hinglish Tip
In Inside something
Big areas (city, country)		 Months, years, seasons
Parts of day		 "In" = Andar ya badi jagah
"In July", "In India"
On Surface par
Lines (road, border)		 Days & dates
Special days		 "On" = Upar ya specific din
"On Monday", "On the table"
At Exact point
Specific places		 Exact time
Special times		 "At" = Exact point
"At 5 PM", "At the door"

2. Since / For (Time)

Since: Starting point batata hai
✔ I have lived here since 2020.
✔ She has been working since morning.

For: Duration batata hai
✔ I have lived here for 3 years.
✔ She has been working for 5 hours.

3. By / With (Method/Instrument)

By: Method or agent (kiske dwara)
✔ I go to office by car.
✔ The book was written by Tagore.

With: Instrument or accompaniment
✔ I cut vegetables with a knife.
✔ She came with her friend.

4. Between / Among

Between: Do cheezon ke beech
✔ The school is between the hospital and the market.
✔ Divide the money between Ram and Shyam.

Among: Kai cheezon ke beech
✔ She was sitting among her friends.
✔ Divide the sweets among all the children.

❌ Common Errors & Mistakes

1. Wrong Preposition After Verbs

Wrong: He is good in mathematics.

Correct: He is good at mathematics.

Why: "Good at" is the correct collocation, not "good in".

2. Literal Hindi → English Translation

Wrong: I am going in market.

Correct: I am going to the market.

Why: Hindi mein "market mein jana" hota hai, but English mein "going to market".

3. Unnecessary Preposition Usage

Wrong: Where are you going to?

Correct: Where are you going?

Why: "Where" already includes the idea of "to", so no need for extra preposition.

4. Wrong Preposition with Time

Wrong: I will meet you in Monday.

Correct: I will meet you on Monday.

Why: Days of the week always use "on", not "in".

5. Confusing Since and For

Wrong: I have been waiting since two hours.

Correct: I have been waiting for two hours.

Why: Use "for" with duration, "since" with starting point.
