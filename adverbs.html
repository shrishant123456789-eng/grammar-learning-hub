ADVERBS

Complete Grammar Chapter for Competitive Exams
Learn • Revise • Practice

Definition: An Adverb is a word that modifies (adds meaning to) a Verb, an Adjective, or another Adverb.

Hinglish: Wo shabd jo Verb (kriya), Adjective (visheshan) ya kisi dusre Adverb ki visheshta bataye ya usme extra jankari jode.

Key Concept:

It answers questions like: How? When? Where? How often? To what extent?

Examples:
  • He runs fast. (Modifies Verb 'runs')
  • She is very beautiful. (Modifies Adjective 'beautiful')
  • He works quite efficiently. (Modifies Adverb 'efficiently')
  • They arrived yesterday. (Time)
  • Please come here. (Place)
  • I always speak the truth. (Frequency)

In exams, ask the verb questions to find the adverb.

Trick: Agar sentence mein Verb se pucho "Kaise?", "Kab?", "Kahan?", "Kitni baar?" - jo jawab mile wo Adverb hai.
Sentence Question Asked Adverb Found
She sings sweetly. How does she sing? (Kaise?) Sweetly
He came late. When did he come? (Kab?) Late
Look up. Where to look? (Kahan?) Up
He often visits us. How often? (Kitni baar?) Often

A. Adverb of Manner (How?)

Shows how an action is done. Usually ends in '-ly'.

Kaam karne ka tarika batata hai.
  • Examples: Slowly, Quickly, Bravely, Hard, Fast, Well.
  • Example: He fought bravely.

B. Adverb of Place (Where?)

Shows the place of action.

Jagah ke baare mein batata hai.
  • Examples: Here, There, Up, Down, Everywhere, Near, Far.
  • Example: God is everywhere.

C. Adverb of Time (When?)

Shows the time of action.

Samay batata hai.
  • Examples: Now, Then, Today, Yesterday, Tomorrow, Soon, Late.
  • Example: I will call you soon.

D. Adverb of Frequency (How often?)

Shows how many times an action occurs.

Kaam kitni baar hota hai.
  • Examples: Always, Never, Often, Seldom, Usually, Once.
  • Example: She never tells a lie.

E. Adverb of Degree (How much?)

Shows the intensity or degree.

Matra ya teevrata batata hai.
  • Examples: Very, Too, Quite, Almost, Enough, So.
  • Example: The tea is too hot.

F. Interrogative Adverbs

Used to ask questions.

  • Examples: When, Where, How, Why.
  • Example: Where do you live?

Correct placement of adverbs is crucial for Spotting Errors.

Rule 1: M-P-T Rule

If Manner, Place, and Time adverbs come together, follow this order: Manner → Place → Time.

Correct: She danced beautifully (M) at the party (P) yesterday (T).
Incorrect: She danced yesterday at the party beautifully. ❌

Rule 2: Adverbs of Frequency

Place them before the Main Verb but after the Helping Verb.

Context Example Sentence Reason
Main Verb only He always comes late. Before Main Verb (comes)
Helping + Main He has never seen the Taj. After Helping (has), Before Main (seen)
Verb 'to be' (is/am/are) He is always happy. After 'is/am/are/was/were'

Rule 3: 'Enough'

Adjective + Enough (Adjective comes before Enough).

Enough + Noun (Noun comes after Enough).

  • He is smart enough to solve this. (Smart = Adj) ✔
  • He has enough money. (Money = Noun) ✔

Like Adjectives, Adverbs also have degrees of comparison.

Positive Comparative Superlative
Fast Faster Fastest
Hard Harder Hardest
Carefully More carefully Most carefully
Beautifully More beautifully Most beautifully
Well Better Best
Badly Worse Worst

Many students confuse Adjectives and Adverbs.

Adjective Noun/Pronoun ke liye hota hai, Adverb Verb ke liye.

Common Pairs:

1. Good vs. Well

  • Good is an Adjective. (He is a good boy.)
  • Well is an Adverb. (He sings well.)

2. Hard vs. Hardly (Exam Trap)

  • Hard: With effort/solid. (He works hard.) - Positive meaning.
  • Hardly: Scarcely/Almost not. (He hardly works.) - Negative meaning.

3. Fast

  • There is NO word like "Fastly".
  • Adj: He is a fast runner.
  • Adv: He runs fast.
Wrong ❌ Correct ✔ Explanation
He runs fastly. He runs fast. 'Fastly' does not exist in English.
I am too happy. I am very happy. 'Too' has a negative sense (more than required). Use 'Very' for positive.
He plays good. He plays well. 'Play' is a verb, needs Adverb 'Well'.
He came directly from school. He came direct from school. Direct = Straight; Directly = Immediately/Soon.
I seldom or ever go there. I seldom or never go there. Pair is 'Seldom or never' or 'Seldom if ever'.
He is enough rich to buy a car. He is rich enough to buy a car. Adj comes BEFORE enough.
She only solved two questions. She solved only two questions. Place 'only' before the word it modifies.
I hardly not know him. I hardly know him. 'Hardly' is negative, don't use 'not' with it.
