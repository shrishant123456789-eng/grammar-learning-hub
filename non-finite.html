Non-Finite Verbs

Master Infinitives, Gerunds & Participles

1. Introduction

What are Non-Finite Verbs?
These verbs do NOT change their form according to the subject (number/person) or tense of the sentence. They act as Nouns, Adjectives, or Adverbs.
Finite Verb (Changes) Non-Finite Verb (No Change)
I like to play. I like to play.
He likes to play. He likes to play.
They liked to play. They liked to play.

*Notice how 'like' changes, but 'to play' remains same.

Non-finite verbs wo verbs hote hain jo subject ya tense ke hisaab se badalte nahi hain. Ye sentence mein verb ka kaam nahi karte, balki Noun ya Adjective ka kaam karte hain.

2. Types of Non-Finite Verbs

1️⃣ INFINITIVE (The 'To' Verb)

Definition

Usually formed by To + V1. It acts like a Noun.

Hindi mein: 'Na', 'Ne', 'Ni' (Jaana, khana, sona).
Ex: I want to go (Main jaana chahta hu).

Types

  • To-Infinitive: To + Base Verb (To walk, To eat).
  • Bare Infinitive: Base Verb without 'To' (used after modals, let, make, see, hear).

Common Mistakes

Wrong ❌Correct ✔Reason
He made me to cry.He made me cry.'Make' takes Bare Infinitive (no 'to').
I look forward to meet you.I look forward to meeting you.'Look forward to' is a phrase taking Gerund, not Infinitive.

2️⃣ GERUND (Verbal Noun)

Definition

Formed by V1 + ing. It looks like a verb but acts as a Noun.

Hindi mein: Iska ant bhi 'Na' se hota hai.
Ex: Swimming is good (Tairna accha hai).

Usage Rules

  • Used after prepositions (of, for, about, in).
  • Used after specific verbs: enjoy, avoid, mind, finish, suggest, keep.

Examples

  • Walking is good for health. (Subject)
  • I like reading. (Object)
  • He is fond of singing. (Object of preposition)

3️⃣ PARTICIPLE (Verbal Adjective)

Definition

Acts as an Adjective or creates verb tenses.

Forms

  • Present Participle (V1+ing): Continuous action.
    Ex: A burning train. (Jalti hui train)
  • Past Participle (V3): Completed action/State.
    Ex: A burnt train. (Jali hui train)
  • Perfect Participle (Having + V3): Action completed before another.
    Ex: Having eaten, he slept.
Hindi mein: 'Ta hua', 'Te huye', ya 'Huye'.
Running water (Behta hua paani). Broken chair (Tooti hui kursi).

3. Gerund vs Infinitive (Tricky Differences)

Some verbs change meaning depending on what follows.

Phrase Meaning
Stop to smoke Stopped doing something else in order to smoke.
Stop smoking Quit the habit of cigarettes forever.
Remember to lock Don't forget the task.
Remember locking Recall the memory of locking it.

4. Dangling Participles (Advanced)

Rule: The subject of the participle phrase must be the same as the subject of the main sentence.

Wrong: Walking down the road, a dog bit him.

(Logic: Was the dog walking down the road? No, the person was.)

Correct: While he was walking down the road, a dog bit him.
