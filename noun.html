1️⃣ WHAT IS A NOUN?

📚 Definition (Simple English)

A noun is the name of a person, place, thing, or idea.

🇮🇳 Hinglish Explanation

Noun wo shabd hota hai jo kisi naam ko batata hai — jaise insaan, jagah, cheez ya feeling.

👤 Person

Rahul is my friend.

The teacher is kind.

📍 Place

I live in Delhi.

The school is big.

🎒 Thing

My book is new.

The car is fast.

💡 Idea

Love is important.

Freedom is precious.

Identification Trick (Naam Test): Kisi bhi shabd ko test karne ke liye pucho "Kya ye kisi naam ko batata hai?" If YES → Noun!

✅ Right vs Wrong Examples

✓ The boy runs fast. (Boy = person = Noun)

✗ The runs fast boy. (Wrong word order)

✓ Mumbai is a big city. (Mumbai = place = Noun)

✗ Mumbai are a big city. (Wrong verb)

2️⃣ TYPES OF NOUN (FULLY DETAILED)

A. Proper Noun (व्यक्तिवाचक संज्ञा)

Definition: A proper noun is the specific name of a particular person, place, or thing. It always begins with a capital letter.

Hinglish: Proper noun kisi vishest vyakti, sthan ya vastu ka naam hota hai. Hamesha bada letter se shuru hota hai.

Identification Trick: Agar naam kisi ek hi cheez ko indicate karta hai (unique) → Proper Noun

Examples:

India is my country.

Delhi is the capital.

Narendra Modi is the PM.

Monday is holiday.

Taj Mahal is beautiful.

Ganga is sacred.

Himalaya is tall.

January is cold.

SSC Warning: Days, months, festivals, languages, religions — sab proper noun hain!

B. Common Noun (जातिवाचक संज्ञा)

Definition: A common noun is a general name for a person, place, or thing of the same class or kind.

Hinglish: Common noun kisi samuh ya jaati ka saadharan naam hota hai.

Identification Trick: Agar naam general hai aur kai cheezon ke liye use ho sakta hai → Common Noun

Examples:

The boy is playing.

A city is big.

The river is long.

My school is good.

The doctor came.

A book is on table.

The car is fast.

That building is tall.

Common Confusion: Boy (common) vs Rahul (proper), City (common) vs Delhi (proper)

C. Collective Noun (समूहवाचक संज्ञा)

Definition: A collective noun is the name of a group or collection of persons or things taken together.

Hinglish: Collective noun kisi samuh ya group ka naam hota hai.

Identification Trick: Agar ek shabd puri team/group ko indicate karta hai → Collective Noun

Examples:

A team of players

A flock of birds

A herd of cattle

A crowd of people

A class of students

A army of soldiers

A bunch of keys

A fleet of ships

SSC Exam Note: Collective noun ke saadharan examples yaad rakho - team, flock, herd, crowd, class, army

D. Material Noun (पदार्थवाचक संज्ञा)

Definition: A material noun is the name of a material or substance of which things are made.

Hinglish: Material noun kisi padaarth ya substance ka naam hota hai jis se cheezein banti hain.

Identification Trick: Agar naam kisi raw material ko indicate karta hai → Material Noun

Examples:

Gold is precious.

Water is essential.

Iron is strong.

Cotton is soft.

Wood comes from trees.

Plastic is light.

Glass is transparent.

Milk is nutritious.

Important: Material nouns hamesha singular hote hain aur inka plural nahin hota!

E. Abstract Noun (भाववाचक संज्ञा)

Definition: An abstract noun is the name of a quality, state, or action that we can only think of or feel.

Hinglish: Abstract noun kisi guna, avastha ya kriya ka naam hota hai jo sirf mehsoos ki ja sakti hai, chhooi nahin.

Identification Trick: Agar naam kisi feeling, idea, quality ya state ko indicate karta hai → Abstract Noun

Examples:

Love is blind.

Honesty is best.

Happiness matters.

Courage is needed.

Childhood is golden.

Freedom is precious.

Wisdom comes with age.

Sleep is necessary.

SSC Focus: -ness, -hood, -ity, -tion endings wale words usually abstract nouns hote hain!

3️⃣ COUNTABLE & UNCOUNTABLE NOUNS

📊 Countable Nouns

Nouns that can be counted (have singular and plural forms)

Hinglish: Aise nouns jinko ginna ja sakta hai

📊 Uncountable Nouns

Nouns that cannot be counted (only singular form)

Hinglish: Aise nouns jinko nahin ginna ja sakta

Aspect Countable Uncountable
Examples book, apple, boy, car water, rice, love, information
Articles a/an for singular no a/an
Plural books, apples no plural
How many/much How many books? How much water?
Some/Any some books, any apples some water, any information

❌ Wrong vs ✔ Correct Examples

❌ I need an information.

✔ I need some information.

❌ How many money do you have?

✔ How much money do you have?

❌ She gave me a good advice.

✔ She gave me good advice.

Hinglish Rule: Much = uncountable, Many = countable. Yaad rakho: "Much money, Many friends"

4️⃣ GENDER OF NOUN

👨 Masculine Gender

Indicates male

boy, man, father, king, hero, actor, lion

👩 Feminine Gender

Indicates female

girl, woman, mother, queen, heroine, actress, lioness

🤝 Common Gender

Can be male or female

teacher, student, friend, doctor, child, person

⚡ Neuter Gender

Non-living things

book, table, pen, car, house, computer

Masculine Feminine Rule
actor actress +ess
hero heroine +ine
prince princess +ess
waiter waitress +ess
lion lioness +ess
Real Exam Example: "The lioness protected her cubs." → lioness = feminine gender

5️⃣ NUMBER (SINGULAR & PLURAL)

Regular Plurals

Rule Singular Plural Example
Add -s book books Most nouns
Add -es box boxes Ends in -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z
Change -y to -ies city cities Consonant + y
Add -s boy boys Vowel + y
Change -f/-fe to -ves knife knives Most -f endings
Add -es potato potatoes Consonant + o

Irregular Plurals

man → men

woman → women

child → children

foot → feet

tooth → teeth

mouse → mice

goose → geese

ox → oxen

Unchanging Nouns

sheep → sheep

deer → deer

fish → fish

aircraft → aircraft

Common Exam Trap: "The sheep is grazing" vs "The sheep are grazing" — dono sahi hain depending on context!

6️⃣ POSSESSION ('s / of)

📖 Apostrophe Rules

Living things: Use 's or s'

Non-living things: Use "of"

🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Living Things ('s)

Rahul's book

The boy's toy

My sister's dress

The cat's tail

📚 Non-living Things (of)

The roof of the house

The leg of the table

The door of the car

The cover of the book

Special Rules

Plural ending in -s → Add only apostrophe (')

✔ The boys' school (many boys)

✔ The girls' hostel (many girls)

Compound possession

✔ Rahul and Priya's house (joint ownership)

✔ Rahul's and Priya's books (separate ownership)

Hinglish Explanation: Jinda cheezon ke liye 's lagao, bina jaan ke liye "of" ka use karo!

7️⃣ COMMON ERRORS & MISTAKES

❌ Wrong Sentence ✔ Correct Sentence Why Wrong?
The sceneries are beautiful. The scenery is beautiful. Scenery is uncountable, no plural
He gave me an advice. He gave me advice. Advice is uncountable
The furnitures are new. The furniture is new. Furniture is uncountable
I have many money. I have much money. Money is uncountable, use 'much'
The news are good. The news is good. News is uncountable, takes singular verb
Sheeps are grazing. Sheep are grazing. Sheep plural is sheep
The childrens are playing. The children are playing. Child plural is children
This is Priya's and Rahul's house. This is Priya and Rahul's house. Joint possession - only last name gets 's
