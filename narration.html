DIRECT & INDIRECT SPEECH

Complete Narration Chapter for Competitive Exams
Learn • Revise • Practice

Narration means the art of reporting the words of a speaker. There are two ways to do this:

1. Direct Speech:
We quote the actual words of the speaker using inverted commas (" ").
Example: He said, "I am busy."
2. Indirect Speech (Reported Speech):
We report what the speaker said without using his exact words. The meaning remains same, but structure changes.
Example: He said that he was busy.
Hinglish Explanation:
Direct: Jab hum kisi ki baat ko bilkul waisa hi bolte hain jaisa usne kaha tha.
Indirect: Jab hum kisi ki baat ko apne shabdon mein badal kar batate hain.

In exams, you need to identify the parts of the sentence to change them correctly.

Trick: "Commas wala part 'Reported Speech' hota hai aur Commas ke bahar wala part 'Reporting Verb' hota hai."
Part Identity Trick Example
Reporting Verb Outside the quotes (" "). Tells WHO said it. She said to me, "..."
Reported Speech Inside the quotes (" "). The ACTUAL dialogue. She said, "I am happy."

Before learning rules, understand these 4 terms:

  • Reporter: The person who speaks (Subject).
  • Reporting Verb (RV): The verb outside commas (e.g., said, told, asks).
  • Reported Speech (RS): The sentence inside inverted commas.
  • Verb of RS: The main verb inside the commas.
Example Breakdown:
He (Reporter) said to (Reporting Verb) me (Object), "I am playing." (Reported Speech)

A. ASSERTIVE SENTENCES (Simple Statements)

  • Connector: Use 'that'.
  • Reporting Verb:
    Said to → Told
    Said → Said (No Change if no object)
Direct: He said to me, "I work hard."
Indirect: He told me that he worked hard.

B. INTERROGATIVE SENTENCES (Questions)

  • Connector:
    Yes/No Question → Use 'if' or 'whether'.
    Wh- Question (What, Why) → Use the SAME Wh-word.
  • Reporting Verb: Said to → Asked / Enquired.
  • Structure: Question becomes a Statement (Subject comes before Verb).
Direct: He said to me, "Are you ill?"
Indirect: He asked me if I was ill. (Not 'was I ill')

Direct: He said, "What are you doing?"
Indirect: He asked what I was doing.

C. IMPERATIVE SENTENCES (Order, Request, Advice)

  • Connector: Use 'to'. (For negative use 'not to').
  • Reporting Verb: Ordered, Requested, Advised, Forbidden.
Direct: The teacher said, "Sit down."
Indirect: The teacher ordered the students to sit down.

D. EXCLAMATORY SENTENCES (Strong Feelings)

  • Connector: Use 'that'.
  • Reporting Verb: Exclaimed with joy / sorrow / surprise.
  • Words Removed: Hurrah, Alas, Wow, Oh.
Direct: He said, "Hurrah! We won."
Indirect: He exclaimed with joy that they had won.

SSC and Banking exams often trick students with these exceptions.

1. Universal Truth / Habitual Action

If the reported speech is a universal truth, NO TENSE CHANGE happens.

Direct: He said, "The sun rises in the east."
Indirect: He said that the sun rises in the east. (Not 'rose')

2. Reporting Verb in Present/Future

If outside verb is 'Says' or 'Will say', NO TENSE CHANGE inside.

Direct: He says, "I am busy."
Indirect: He says that he is busy. (Not 'was')
Wrong ❌ Correct ✔ Reason
He asked me that if I was ready. He asked me if I was ready. Never use double connectors ('that' + 'if').
He told to me that... He told me that... 'Told' never takes 'to'. 'Said to' becomes 'Told'.
He asked where was I going. He asked where I was going. In indirect, Question becomes Statement (Subj + Verb).

Follow these 5 steps to solve any narration question:

  1. Identify Type: Is it Assertive, Interrogative, or Imperative?
  2. Change RV: Change 'said to' → told/asked/ordered.
  3. Add Connector: Add that/if/to/wh-word.
  4. Apply SON Rule: Change Pronouns (I→He, You→Me etc).
  5. Change Tense & Time: Present→Past, Now→Then.
Bas ye 5 steps dimag mein rakho, question kabhi galat nahi hoga.
