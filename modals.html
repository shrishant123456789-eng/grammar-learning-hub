MODALS

Complete Grammar Chapter for Competitive Exams
Learn • Revise • Practice

Definition: Modals (or Modal Verbs) are helper verbs that express the mood or attitude of the speaker. They do not work as a main verb alone.

Hinglish: Ye wo verbs hote hain jo speaker ka mood (bhaav) ya attitude dikhate hain. Jaise ability, permission, request, ya duty.

List of Modals:

Can, Could, May, Might, Will, Would, Shall, Should, Must, Ought to, Need, Dare, Used to.

GOLDEN RULE:
Subject + Modal + V1 (Base Form of Verb)
Example: He can go. (Not 'goes', not 'going')

Examples:

  • I can lift this box. (Ability)
  • It may rain today. (Possibility)
  • You should work hard. (Advice)
  • He must apologize. (Compulsion)
  • May I come in? (Permission)
  • I will help you. (Promise)

How to identify if a word is a Modal or a Normal Verb?

TRICK: Agar kisi verb ke baad kabhi 's/es', 'ing', ya 'ed' nahi lagta, to wo MODAL hai.
Sentence Modal Meaning / Use
I can swim. Can Mental/Physical Ability
You must go. Must Strong Compulsion (Zaroori)
It might rain. Might Low Possibility (Kam sambhavna)

Note: We never say "Canning", "Musted", or "Cans". This is the easiest way to identify them.

A. CAN

Uses: Ability (Shamta), Permission (Informal), Possibility.

  • I can speak English. (Ability)
  • Can I take your pen? (Informal Permission - Friends/Family)
  • Fire can be dangerous. (General Possibility)

B. COULD

Uses: Past Ability, Polite Request, Possibility.

  • I could run fast when I was young. (Past Ability)
  • Could you help me, please? (Very Polite Request)
  • He could be at home. (Possibility)

C. MAY

Uses: Permission (Formal), Strong Possibility, Wish/Blessing.

  • May I come in, Sir? (Formal Permission)
  • It may rain today. (High chance - 50%+)
  • May God bless you! (Wish)

D. MIGHT

Uses: Weak Possibility (Kam sambhavna).

  • He might come, but I am not sure. (Low chance - 20%)
  • I might buy a car next year.

E. WILL

Uses: Future, Promise, Determination, Instant Decision.

  • I will call you. (Future)
  • I will clear the exam this time. (Determination)

F. WOULD

Uses: Polite Request, Past Habit, Imaginary situation.

  • Would you like some tea? (Offer/Request)
  • He would go for a walk daily. (Past Habit)
  • If I were a bird, I would fly. (Imaginary)

G. SHALL

Uses: Suggestion, Offer (Used mostly with I/We in questions).

  • Shall we go now? (Suggestion)
  • Shall I carry your bag? (Offer)

H. SHOULD

Uses: Advice, Duty, Moral Obligation.

  • You should study hard. (Advice)
  • We should respect our elders. (Moral Duty)

I. MUST

Uses: Strong Obligation, Compulsion, Logical Assumption.

  • You must wear a helmet. (Compulsion/Law)
  • He has a big car; he must be rich. (Assumption)

J. OUGHT TO

Uses: Moral Duty (Similar to Should but more formal).

  • We ought to love our country.
  • You ought to help the poor.
Rule 1: Always use V1 (Base form) after Modals.
Rule 2: Do not use "to" after Modals (Except 'Ought to' & 'Used to').
Wrong ❌ Correct ✔ Reason
He can goes there. He can go there. Modals don't take s/es.
She will coming soon. She will come soon. Modals don't take 'ing'.
You should to study. You should study. No 'to' after should.
He cann't do it. He cannot / can't do it. Spelling error.

Structure: Subject + Modal + NOT + V1

Hinglish: Modal ke turant baad 'NOT' lagana hai, aur fir V1.

Short Forms (Contractions):

  • Cannot → Can't
  • Will not → Won't (Important)
  • Shall not → Shan't
  • Must not → Mustn't
  • Should not → Shouldn't

Example:
You should not (shouldn't) smoke.
She will not (won't) come today.

Structure: Modal + Subject + V1 + ...?

Hinglish: Sawal puchne ke liye Modal ko Subject se pehle rakhte hain.
  • Can you drive a car?
  • May I use your phone?
  • Should I call him?
  • Will you marry me?
  • Could you pass the salt?
  • Must we go there?

These are the most common mistakes asked in SSC and Banking exams.

Wrong ❌ Correct ✔ Explanation
He can able to do this. He can do this. / He is able to do this. 'Can' and 'Able to' both mean ability. Don't use together (Superfluous).
You should to go. You should go. 'To' comes only with 'Ought' and 'Used'.
He needs work hard. He needs to work hard. Here 'Needs' is a main verb, not modal.
If it will rain, I will not come. If it rains, I will not come. Double Future is wrong. 1st part Present Indefinite.
She must has done it. She must have done it. After Modal, always use base form 'Have', never 'Has'.
