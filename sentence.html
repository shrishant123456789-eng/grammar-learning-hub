SENTENCE

Complete Grammar Chapter for Competitive Exams

1. WHAT IS A SENTENCE?

Definition: A group of words that makes complete sense and expresses a complete thought is called a Sentence.

Hinglish: Shabdon ka aisa group jiska pura arth (complete meaning) nikle, use Sentence kahte hain. Agar meaning complete nahi hai, to wo sentence nahi hai.

Formula: Subject + Verb + Object/Complement

Right: Ram goes to the market. (Complete sense)
Wrong: Market to goes Ram. (No sense)
Wrong: In the garden. (Incomplete sense)

Key Elements:

  • It must begin with a Capital Letter.
  • It must end with a Full Stop (.), Question Mark (?), or Exclamation Mark (!).
  • It must have at least one Subject and one Verb.
2. IDENTIFICATION TRICK (PAHCHAN)

Exam mein sentence identify karne ke liye sabse pehle Verb dhundhe aur puche "Kaun kar raha hai?" (Subject).

Trick: Kya ye line padhne ke baad aapke dimaag mein ek puri picture ban rahi hai? Agar haan, to ye sentence hai. Agar sawaal bacha rah jaye, to ye sentence nahi hai.
Group of Words Question to ask Result
Playing cricket. Who is playing? (Answer nahi mila) ❌ Not a Sentence
He is playing cricket. Who? (He). What doing? (Playing). ✔ Sentence
Because it was raining. To kya hua? (Incomplete thought) ❌ Not a Sentence
3. TYPES OF SENTENCE (BY PURPOSE)

There are 4 main types of sentences based on what they do (Purpose).

A. Assertive / Declarative Sentence

These sentences state a fact, opinion, or idea. They simply declare something.

Ye simple waakya hote hain jo koi jaankari dete hain. Ye hamesha Full Stop (.) par khatam hote hain.
The sun rises in the east. (Fact)
Rohit is a good player. (Opinion)

B. Interrogative Sentence

These sentences ask a question.

Inmein koi sawaal pucha jata hai. Last mein Question Mark (?) lagana zaroori hai.
Where do you live?
Are you hungry?

C. Imperative Sentence

These sentences express a command, request, advice, or suggestion.

Isme Subject 'You' aksar chupa (hidden) hota hai. Jaise "Sit down" ka matlab hai "(You) sit down".
Shut the door. (Order)
Please help me. (Request)
Take medicine on time. (Advice)

D. Exclamatory Sentence

These sentences express strong emotion (joy, sorrow, surprise, anger).

Achanak nikli hui feelings ko batane ke liye use hota hai. Exclamation Mark (!) zaroori hai.
What a beautiful flower!
Hurrah! We won the match.
4. TYPES OF SENTENCE (BY STRUCTURE)

Structure (banawat) ke aadhar par sentences 3 tarah ke hote hain.

Type Definition Example
Simple Sentence One Subject + One Finite Verb.
(Sirf ek baat kahi gayi ho).		 Birds fly in the sky.
Compound Sentence Two independent clauses joined by Conjunctions (FANBOYS: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So).
(Do barabar ke waakya jude ho).		 I like tea but my brother likes coffee.
Complex Sentence One Independent clause + One or more Dependent clauses.
(Ek main baat, aur dusri uspar nirbhar).		 I know that he is honest.
5. AFFIRMATIVE, NEGATIVE & INTERROGATIVE RULES

Conversion Rules (Badalne ke niyam)

1. Affirmative to Negative: Add 'not' after the helping verb. If no helping verb, use Do/Does/Did.

Simple Present mein 'Does not/Do not' aur Simple Past mein 'Did not' lagayein.
She is playing. → She is not playing.
He likes apples. → He does not like apples. (Note: 'likes' becomes 'like')
They went home. → They did not go home. (Note: 'went' becomes 'go')

2. Affirmative to Interrogative: Bring the Helping Verb to the front.

He is happy. → Is he happy?
You play cricket. → Do you play cricket?
6. SUBJECT & PREDICATE

Every complete sentence has two parts:

1. Subject (Uddeshya): The person or thing we are talking about. (Doer of the action).

2. Predicate (Vidhey): What is said about the Subject. It always contains the Verb.

Sentence mein "Kaun" (Who) lagane se Subject milta hai, baaki bacha hua hissa Predicate hota hai.
Sentence Subject (Who/What?) Predicate (What happened?)
The dog barks loudly. The dog barks loudly.
My best friend is coming. My best friend is coming.
On the top of the hill lives a hermit. A hermit
(Sometimes subject comes last)		 lives on the top of the hill.
7. COMMON ERRORS & MISTAKES

Competitive exams mein aane wali common galtiyan:

❌ Wrong Sentence ✔ Correct Sentence Reason (Kyun?)
He does not goes to school. He does not go to school. Do/Does/Did ke baad hamesha Verb ki 1st form aati hai (s/es nahi lagta).
I no have money. I do not have money. / I have no money. Verb se pehle 'no' nahi, 'do not' lagta hai.
Why you are crying? Why are you crying? Question mein Helping verb (are) Subject (you) se pehle aani chahiye.
She did not wrote a letter. She did not write a letter. Did ke saath V2 nahi, V1 use hota hai.
