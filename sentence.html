SENTENCE – Complete Grammar Chapter

Master the foundation of English grammar with comprehensive sentence analysis

📚 What is a Sentence?

Definition

A sentence is a group of words that makes complete sense. It expresses a complete thought and can stand alone.

Sentence shabdon ka group hota hai jo complete meaning deta ho. Ye ek complete thought express karta hai aur khud mein poora hota hai.
BASIC FORMULA: Subject + Verb + Object

Right vs Wrong Examples:

✔ Correct Sentences:
• Ram eats an apple.
• She is reading a book.
• They play cricket daily.
• The sun rises in the east.
❌ Not Sentences:
• Ram an apple (no verb)
• Reading a book (no subject)
• Play cricket daily (no subject)
• In the east (no subject-verb)
Exam Tip: Sentence mein subject aur verb dono hone chahiye. Agar koi missing hai, to wo sentence nahi hai.

🔍 Sentence Identification Tricks

"Who + What Doing" Trick

Ask these two questions to any group of words:

  1. Who? (Subject identification)
  2. What doing? (Verb identification)
Kisi bhi shabd group pe ye do sawal pucho: Kaun? Kya kar raha hai? Dono jawab mile to sentence hai.

Complete Thought Rule

A sentence must express a complete idea that doesn't leave the reader asking questions.

Group of Words Who? What doing? Sentence?
Ram eats an apple. Ram eats ✔ Yes
On the table ❌ None ❌ None ❌ No
She is reading She is reading ✔ Yes
Playing cricket ❌ None Playing ❌ No

🎯 Types of Sentences (By Purpose)

A) Assertive Sentences (Statements) ▼

What are Assertive Sentences?

These sentences make a statement or declaration. They give information about something.

Ye sentence kisi baat ko batate hain ya declare karte hain. Ye information dete hain.

Characteristics:

  • End with a full stop (.)
  • Can be positive or negative
  • Most common type of sentence

Examples:

Positive:
• I like tea.
• She is a teacher.
• They play cricket.
• The sun rises in the east.

Negative:
• I don't like coffee.
• She is not a doctor.
• They don't play football.
• The moon doesn't shine itself.
B) Interrogative Sentences (Questions) ▼

What are Interrogative Sentences?

These sentences ask questions. They seek information.

Ye sentence sawal puchte hain. Ye information mangte hain.

Characteristics:

  • End with a question mark (?)
  • Start with helping verbs or question words
  • Word order: Helping verb + Subject + Main verb

Types & Examples:

Yes/No Questions:
• Are you coming?
• Do you like tea?
• Can she swim?
• Did they go?

WH-Questions:
• What is your name?
• Where do you live?
• When will you come?
• Why are you late?
• How are you feeling?
C) Imperative Sentences (Commands/Requests) ▼

What are Imperative Sentences?

These sentences give commands, make requests, or offer advice.

Ye sentence order dete hain, request karte hain, ya advice dete hain.

Characteristics:

  • Usually end with a full stop (.)
  • Can end with exclamation mark (!) for strong commands
  • Subject 'you' is usually hidden
  • Start with base form of verb

Examples:

Commands:
• Sit down.
• Open the door.
• Don't be late.
• Clean your room.

Requests:
• Please help me.
• Could you pass the salt?
• Kindly wait here.
• Would you mind sitting?

Advice:
• Take care of your health.
• Study regularly.
• Don't waste time.
D) Exclamatory Sentences (Emotions/Feelings) ▼

What are Exclamatory Sentences?

These sentences express strong emotions, feelings, or surprise.

Ye sentence strong emotions, feelings ya surprise express karte hain.

Characteristics:

  • End with an exclamation mark (!)
  • Often start with 'What' or 'How'
  • Express joy, sorrow, surprise, anger, etc.

Examples:

With 'What':
• What a beautiful day!
• What a surprise!
• What an amazing performance!
• What a lovely dress!

With 'How':
• How beautiful the moon is!
• How clever you are!
• How fast he runs!
• How delicious this food is!

Other Exclamations:
• Alas! He is no more.
• Hurrah! We won the match.
• Oh! What a pity.

🏗️ Types of Sentences (By Structure)

Type Definition Structure Examples
Simple One independent clause Subject + Verb + Object • I eat an apple.
• She is reading.
• They play cricket.
Compound Two independent clauses joined by conjunction Clause + Conjunction + Clause • I eat an apple and she drinks tea.
• He is poor but honest.
Complex One main clause + one subordinate clause Main Clause + Subordinate Clause • I will go if you come with me.
• She cried because she was sad.
SSC Exam Tip: Simple sentence = ek hi clause, Compound = do independent clauses, Complex = main + subordinate clause

🔄 Affirmative, Negative & Interrogative Rules

Step-by-Step Conversion Rules

1. Present Tense (Simple)

Affirmative: I play cricket.
Negative: I do not play cricket.
Interrogative: Do I play cricket?

2. Present Tense (He/She/It)

Affirmative: He plays cricket.
Negative: He does not play cricket.
Interrogative: Does he play cricket?

3. Past Tense

Affirmative: I played cricket.
Negative: I did not play cricket.
Interrogative: Did I play cricket?
Important Rule: Jab bhi "do/does/did" aaye, main verb hamesha first form mein rahegi!

👥 Subject & Predicate

What is Subject?

The subject is who or what the sentence is about. It tells us who is performing the action.

Subject wo hai jiske baare mein sentence hai. Ye batata hai ki action kaun kar raha hai.

What is Predicate?

The predicate tells us something about the subject. It includes the verb and everything after it.

Predicate subject ke baare mein kuch batata hai. Isme verb aur uske baad ki sab cheezein aati hain.
Sentence Subject Predicate
Ram eats an apple. Ram eats an apple
The beautiful girl is singing. The beautiful girl is singing
My brother and I play cricket. My brother and I play cricket

Special Cases:

Hidden Subject (Imperative):
• (You) Sit down. → Subject 'You' is hidden
• (You) Please help me. → Subject 'You' is hidden

Subject Comes Last:
• There is a book on the table.
• Here comes the bus.

❌ Common Errors & Mistakes

1. Double Negatives

Wrong: I don't know nothing.

Correct: I don't know anything.

Why: Hindi mein "mujhe kuch nahi pata" sahi hai, but English mein double negative galat hai. Use "don't + anything" instead of "don't + nothing".

2. Wrong Word Order in Questions

Wrong: What you are doing?

Correct: What are you doing?

Why: Hindi mein "Tum kya kar rahe ho?" but English mein helping verb (are) subject (you) se pehle aata hai in WH-questions.

3. Missing Articles

Wrong: She is teacher.

Correct: She is a teacher.

Why: Single countable nouns ke pehle article (a/an) lagana zaroori hai.

4. Wrong Preposition with Time

Wrong: I will meet you in 5 PM.

Correct: I will meet you at 5 PM.

Why: Exact time ke saath hamesha "at" use karte hain, "in" nahi.

5. Subject-Verb Agreement Error

Wrong: He don't like tea.

Correct: He doesn't like tea.

Why: He/She/It ke saath "doesn't" aata hai, "don't" nahi.
