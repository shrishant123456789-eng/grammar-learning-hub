Conjunctions (Joining Words)

Master the art of connecting words, phrases, and clauses

📚 Introduction

What are Conjunctions?

Conjunctions are words that connect other words, phrases, or clauses together. They act like glue that holds different parts of a sentence together.

Conjunction shabd doosre shabdon ya vakyon ko jodne ka kaam karte hain. Ye sentence ke different parts ko connect karne mein madad karte hain.
Example: I like tea and coffee. (Here 'and' connects tea and coffee)

🔧 Types of Conjunctions

A) Coordinating Conjunctions (FANBOYS) ▼

What are Coordinating Conjunctions?

These conjunctions connect words, phrases, or clauses of equal importance. They are easy to remember with the acronym FANBOYS.

Ye conjunctions equal importance wale shabdon ya vakyon ko jodte hain. FANBOYS se yaad rakhna easy hai.
F - For
A - And
N - Nor
B - But
O - Or
Y - Yet
S - So

Rules & Usage:

  • For: Shows reason (kyunki)
  • And: Adds information (aur)
  • Nor: Negative alternative (na hi)
  • But: Shows contrast (lekin)
  • Or: Shows choice (ya)
  • Yet: Shows contrast (phir bhi)
  • So: Shows result (isliye)

Examples:

For: I cannot come, for I am busy.
And: She is smart and beautiful.
Nor: He neither smokes nor drinks.
But: I tried hard, but failed.
Or: Would you like tea or coffee?
Yet: He is poor, yet honest.
So: It was raining, so we stayed home.
B) Subordinating Conjunctions ▼

What are Subordinating Conjunctions?

These conjunctions connect a dependent (subordinate) clause to an independent (main) clause. They show relationship between ideas.

Ye conjunctions dependent clause ko main clause se jodte hain. Ye ideas ke beech relationship dikhate hain.

Common Subordinating Conjunctions:

Time: when, while, before, after, since, until, as soon as
Reason: because, since, as
Condition: if, unless, provided that
Contrast: although, though, even though, whereas
Purpose: so that, in order that
Result: so...that, such...that

Rules & Usage:

  • Always connect dependent clause to main clause
  • Can come at the beginning or middle of sentence
  • When at beginning, use comma after dependent clause

Examples:

Time: I will call you when I reach home.
Reason: She failed because she didn't study.
Condition: If it rains, we will cancel the trip.
Contrast: Although he is rich, he is not happy.
Purpose: Work hard so that you can succeed.
Result: He spoke so fast that I couldn't understand.
C) Correlative Conjunctions ▼

What are Correlative Conjunctions?

These are pairs of conjunctions that work together to connect equal elements in a sentence. They always come in pairs.

Ye conjunctions hamesha pairs mein aate hain aur equal elements ko jodte hain.

Common Correlative Conjunction Pairs:

Either - Or
Dono mein se ek
Neither - Nor
Koi nahi
Both - And
Dono
Not only - But also
Keval nahin, balki
Whether - Or
Chahe...ya
Such - That
Aisa...ki

Rules & Usage:

  • Always use in pairs - never use only one part
  • Connect similar grammatical elements
  • Maintain parallel structure
  • Place immediately before the elements they connect

Examples:

Either-Or: Either come today or come tomorrow.
Neither-Nor: He is neither tall nor short.
Both-And: She is both smart and hardworking.
Not only-But also: He not only sings but also dances.
Whether-Or: I don't know whether to go or stay.
Such-That: It was such a hot day that we stayed inside.

❌ Common Mistakes

1. Using Two Conjunctions Together

Wrong: Because he is rich, so he can buy anything.

Correct: Because he is rich, he can buy anything.

Why: Never use two conjunctions together. Use either "because" or "so", not both.

2. Wrong Pair in Correlative Conjunctions

Wrong: Either you come today nor tomorrow.

Correct: Either you come today or tomorrow.

Why: Correlative conjunctions must be used in correct pairs. "Either" always pairs with "or".

3. Sentence Imbalance

Wrong: She both likes dancing and to sing.

Correct: She both likes dancing and singing.

Why: Maintain parallel structure - both parts should be in same grammatical form.

4. Clause Confusion

Wrong: Although he is tired, but he continues working.

Correct: Although he is tired, he continues working.

Why: Don't use "but" with "although". One conjunction is enough to show contrast.
